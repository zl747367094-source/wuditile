How Many Resin Roof Tiles Fit in a 40HQ Container? A Buyer's Loading Guide

By Henan Wudi New Materials Co., Ltd. · November 2026 · 5 min read

Before ordering your first container of ASA synthetic resin roof tiles, you need to plan two things: how much roof area a container covers, and how thickness and packing change that number. Here is how it works in practice.

The two containers most buyers use

ContainerInternal volumeTypical use
20GP~33 m³Test order, or a first order for one city/distributor
40HQ (high cube)~76 m³The standard choice for wholesale — best cost per m²

Quantity depends on three things

1. Thickness

Our tiles run from 1.8 mm to 5 mm. Thickness changes weight per m² — for example, the 880 series weighs about 4.2 kg/m² at 2.5 mm and 5.0 kg/m² at 3.0 mm. The thicker the tile, the fewer m² a container holds before hitting the road weight limit.

2. Packing method

  • Loose-packed bundles (no pallets): maximum quantity per container, most common for full-container orders.
  • Palletized: easier unloading and warehouse handling, but roughly 15–25% fewer tiles fit in the same container.

3. Mixed models and colors

You can mix models and colors in one container. A typical wholesale order is one main model (say 1050) in two or three colors, plus ridge tiles and accessories to match. Mixing uses space slightly less efficiently than a single-model load — plan around 5–10% less area.

As a rule of thumb for quoting your local market: one 40HQ of 2.5 mm tiles covers roughly 40–55 average-size house roofs (about 110 m² of roof area per house). That is the number distributors like to hear.

How to place the order

  1. Tell us the model (880 / 1050 / 900 / 1035), thickness, colors and target roof area — or just send us the quantity you can sell per month.
  2. We reply with a quotation, an exact container loading plan and free samples.

  4. Before shipping we send loading photos and video, so you can see exactly how your container was packed.
First order tip: if you are unsure what your market will take, start with a 20GP mixing two colors of one model plus matching ridge tiles. It is enough stock to test retail demand, and the second container can be planned with real sales data.
